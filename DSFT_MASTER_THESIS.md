## Executive Summary

This thesis presents **Dynamic Semantic Field Theory (DSFT)** , a mathematical-computational framework for analyzing and classifying dialogues based on the dynamics of interacting forces within the semantic field.

Unlike traditional classification systems that rely on:

- Vectors ❌
- Basins ❌

- 🔷 **Analytical Pressure** (TECHNICAL)

These forces are not independent; they interact with each other through a coupling matrix, creating a **dynamic semantic field** that can be measured and observed.

## Theoretical Framework

### 2.1 The Fundamental Problem

Traditional dialogue classification systems suffer from three main problems:

| Problem | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Overlap** | Different dialogue types classified into same category |
| **Collapse** | All dialogues collapse into single pattern |
| **Zero Stability** | System stable but unmeasurable |
| **No Early Detection** | Cannot predict transitions before they occur |

### 2.2 Core Hypothesis

> **Meaning is not a point in space; it is the dynamics of interaction between opposing forces.**

Every dialogue produces a **tension field** between four fundamental forces.

### 2.3 Semantic Force Definitions (Operational Constructs)

| Force | Symbol | Meaning | Markers |
|-------|--------|---------|---------|
| **Analytical Pressure** | \(F_A\) | Logical pressure, rational thinking | jacobian, eigenvalues, therefore |
| **Exploratory Expansion** | \(F_E\) | Probabilistic expansion, exploration | perhaps, maybe, explore |
| **Affective Resonance** | \(F_R\) | Emotional resonance, affect | concern, danger, crisis |
| **Persuasive Drift** | \(F_P\) | Persuasive drift, guidance | guaranteed, prove, customers |

*These are operational constructs for modeling, not claims about human cognition.*
